## Pre-match diagnostic

Gameweek 5 was the final Gameweek in the initial five-week prospective evaluation of the frozen Model2_v1.0 production model.

The model was not modified in response to GW1-GW4. This preserved a clean prospective benchmark across 50 Premier League matches before the first formal post-launch champion/challenger review.

As in previous Gameweeks, the predicted 1X2 outcome and most likely individual scoreline measure different things. The predicted outcome is determined by summing the probabilities of every possible scoreline that produces a home win, draw or away win. The modal scoreline is simply the single scoreline with the highest individual probability.

A 1-1 modal scoreline can therefore coexist with a home or away win as the predicted 1X2 outcome.

## Pre-match scoreline concentration

Scoreline concentration remained an important diagnostic entering GW5.

Across the first 40 live predictions, **33 of 40** fixtures had 1-1 as their modal scoreline. Only **4 of the first 40 actual results** finished 1-1.

GW5 showed some movement away from the extreme concentration seen in GW4:

- **8 of 10** fixtures had 1-1 as the modal scoreline.
- Newcastle vs Hull City had a modal scoreline of **2-0**.
- Man City vs Sunderland had a modal scoreline of **1-0**.

The expected-goal estimates also showed greater dispersion than GW4. Newcastle had the highest estimated xG at **2.19**, while Hull City had the lowest at **0.90**.

This was an observation rather than evidence that xG compression was the cause of the previous scoreline concentration. GW5 therefore provided another untouched prospective sample for the post-GW5 diagnostic work.

## What we learned

### 1. The live gap remains despite the GW5 recovery

GW5 itself was considerably stronger than GW3 and GW4, with 50% accuracy and probability metrics better than Model 2's historical benchmark.

However, the complete five-Gameweek prospective benchmark remains weaker than historical validation:

- Live accuracy: **42.0%** vs historical **52.69%**
- Live Log Loss: **1.0598** vs historical **0.9927**
- Live Brier Score: **0.6371** vs historical **0.5927**

The deep dive therefore needs to explain both the overall live gap and the substantial variation between Gameweeks.

### 2. Draw discrimination is the clearest repeated behavioural signal

Across 50 live fixtures, **16 actual matches were draws**, but Model 2 selected **zero draws** as its highest-probability outcome.

This is now a stronger hypothesis than it was after GW4 because the behaviour persisted through the complete frozen evaluation.

The investigation must separate **calibration** from **discrimination**. Historical work already indicated that simply increasing draw probabilities does not necessarily improve the model.

### 3. Modal scoreline concentration remains extreme

Model 2 selected 1-1 as the single most likely scoreline in **41 of 50 fixtures**, while only **5 of 50 actual matches** finished 1-1.

GW5 showed slightly more scoreline diversity than GW4, but the five-Gameweek pattern remains substantial.

The deep dive should test whether this is caused by compressed expected-goal estimates, insufficient team-strength differentiation, the Poisson formulation itself, or another feature/model limitation.

### 4. Goal modelling deserves separate attention from 1X2 performance

GW5 demonstrates why these should not be treated as the same problem.

Model 2 achieved 50% 1X2 accuracy and good probability scores while producing only one exact score and a total-goals MAE of 1.732.

The post-GW5 work should therefore examine both the underlying goal estimates and the translation from those estimates into H/D/A probabilities.

### 5. Cold starts are not the only problem

Promoted-team performance varied substantially across the five Gameweeks, but GW5 produced 2/3 correct with strong probability metrics.

Cold-start methodology remains a legitimate experiment area, but the evidence points to broader model behaviour rather than a problem isolated to promoted teams.

### 6. Model 2 still contains useful predictive information

Model 2 should not be discarded simply because the 50-match headline accuracy is below its historical benchmark.

In GW5 it matched the simple baselines on accuracy while outperforming the probabilistic historical-frequency baseline on both Log Loss and Brier Score.

The objective of the next phase is therefore to identify and improve specific weaknesses while retaining useful signal.

## Post-GW5 deep dive

The initial five-Gameweek freeze is now complete. Model2_v1.0 becomes the fixed production benchmark against which subsequent challengers can be measured.

The first improvement sprint will begin with diagnosis rather than selecting a new algorithm in advance.

The leading evidence-driven hypotheses are:

1. **Draw discrimination** - Model 2 assigns meaningful probability to draws but fails to differentiate fixtures strongly enough for draws to become the highest-probability outcome.
2. **Expected-goal and scoreline dispersion** - attacking and defensive estimates may be excessively compressed, contributing to the concentration of 1-1 modal scorelines.
3. **Current team-strength adaptation** - historical features may not respond quickly enough to genuine changes in team quality early in a new season.
4. **Goal-model specification** - the current Poisson-based translation from expected goals into score and outcome probabilities may impose limitations beyond the underlying features.
5. **Promoted-team cold starts** - translation of Championship information into Premier League priors should be tested independently rather than assumed to be the principal issue.
6. **Confidence and disagreement segments** - errors may concentrate in particular probability bands, close-call fixtures or matches where Model 2 disagrees with stronger external signals.

These are hypotheses to test, not predetermined explanations.

## Champion/challenger operating model

From this point, Football Copilot moves from a fixed five-Gameweek observation period to an incremental model-improvement cycle:

**Live performance → insight → hypothesis → targeted experiment → historical/walk-forward/OOT validation → promotion decision → next-Gameweek deployment → live measurement**

Historical seasons can be reconstructed at equivalent early-season checkpoints to increase sample size while preserving temporal integrity.

Where multiple evidence-backed hypotheses are practical, **1-3 targeted experiments** can be run within an iteration rather than artificially restricting development to one model change.

Challengers will be assessed primarily using:

- 1X2 accuracy
- Log Loss
- Brier Score
- hypothesis-specific diagnostics

A challenger must demonstrate sufficiently robust improvement across appropriate historical, walk-forward and out-of-time evidence before replacing the champion.

If no challenger clears that standard, Model 2 remains champion for the next Gameweek. Retaining the champion is a valid experimental result, but the development cycle continues immediately rather than beginning another five-week passive observation period.
