## Pre-match diagnostic

Gameweek 5 is the final Gameweek in the initial five-week prospective evaluation of the frozen Model2_v1.0 production model.

The model has not been modified in response to the first four Gameweeks. This preserves a clean prospective benchmark across approximately 50 Premier League matches before the first formal post-launch champion/challenger review.

As in previous Gameweeks, the predicted 1X2 outcome and most likely individual scoreline measure different things. The predicted outcome is determined by summing the probabilities of every possible scoreline that produces a home win, draw or away win. The modal scoreline is simply the single scoreline with the highest individual probability.

A 1-1 modal scoreline can therefore coexist with a home or away win as the predicted 1X2 outcome.

## Pre-match scoreline concentration

Scoreline concentration remains an important diagnostic entering GW5.

Across the first 40 live predictions, **33 of 40** fixtures had 1-1 as their modal scoreline. Only **4 of the first 40 actual results** finished 1-1.

GW5 shows some movement away from the extreme concentration seen in GW4:

- **8 of 10** fixtures have 1-1 as the modal scoreline.
- Newcastle vs Hull City has a modal scoreline of **2-0**.
- Man City vs Sunderland has a modal scoreline of **1-0**.

The underlying expected-goal estimates also show greater dispersion than GW4. Newcastle has the highest estimated xG at **2.19**, while Hull City has the lowest at **0.90**.

This does not establish that xG compression is the cause of the previous scoreline concentration. It provides another prospective observation that can be tested in the post-GW5 diagnostic work.

## Draw behaviour

Model 2 again predicts **zero draws** as the highest-probability 1X2 outcome in GW5.

Across the first four completed Gameweeks:

- **0 of 40** Model 2 predictions were draws.
- **14 of 40** actual matches were draws.

The model nevertheless assigns meaningful probability to draws in individual fixtures. GW5 draw probabilities range from **19.1% to 26.1%**.

This distinction is important. Previous historical analysis suggested that aggregate draw probability was reasonably calibrated, while the model struggled to discriminate which individual fixtures were most likely to finish as draws.

GW5 provides a final untouched set of live observations before this behaviour is investigated formally.

## Why Model 2 remains frozen for GW5

Changing Model 2 before GW5 would contaminate the original prospective experiment.

The purpose of the five-Gameweek freeze is not to assume that Model 2 is performing adequately. It is to establish a fixed production benchmark against which subsequent challengers can be evaluated.

GW5 therefore remains an untouched Model2_v1.0 prediction set.

The model-development process does not need to remain passive after this point. Once GW5 is complete, the frozen benchmark will contain approximately 50 genuinely prospective predictions and the project will move into an incremental champion/challenger operating model.

## Post-GW5 deep-dive plan

The first post-launch improvement sprint will start from observed evidence rather than selecting a new algorithm in advance.

The leading areas to investigate are currently:

1. **Draw discrimination** - why meaningful draw probabilities consistently fail to become the highest-probability 1X2 outcome.
2. **Expected-goal and scoreline dispersion** - whether attacking and defensive estimates are excessively compressed towards similar values, contributing to the concentration of 1-1 modal predictions.
3. **Current team-strength adaptation** - whether Model 2 responds quickly enough to changes in team strength at the beginning of a new season.
4. **Promoted-team cold starts** - whether the translation of Championship performance into Premier League priors remains appropriate.
5. **Probability confidence and market disagreement** - whether model errors concentrate in particular confidence bands or where Model 2 disagrees materially with stronger external signals.

GW5 evidence will determine which of these become formal experiments. The diagnosis will come before the choice of algorithm.

## Champion/challenger cycle after GW5

After GW5 the development loop becomes:

**Live performance → insight → hypothesis → targeted experiment → historical/walk-forward/OOT validation → promotion decision → next-Gameweek deployment → live measurement**

Where the evidence supports multiple feasible hypotheses, **1-3 targeted challenger experiments** may be tested in the sprint.

A challenger will not be promoted simply because it improves one metric on one dataset. Evidence will be considered across **1X2 accuracy, Log Loss and Brier Score**, together with the diagnostic measure relevant to the hypothesis being tested.

Historical seasons can also be recreated at equivalent early-season checkpoints to increase the evidence available without contaminating the 2026/27 live benchmark.

The objective is to have a materially stronger challenger available for **GW6** if the evidence supports promotion. If no challenger demonstrates sufficiently robust improvement, Model 2 remains champion and the next iteration continues from the strongest unresolved hypothesis.
