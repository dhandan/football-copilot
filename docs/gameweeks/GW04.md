\## Pre-match diagnostic



\### Why does Model 2 predict a winner when the most likely score is 1-1?



All 10 GW4 fixtures have 1-1 as their most likely individual scoreline.  At the same time, Model 2 predicts a home or away win in every fixture.



This looks contradictory, but the two outputs answer different questions.



Take Crystal Palace v Ipswich as a practical example:



\- Crystal Palace win: \*\*43.9%\*\*

\- Draw: \*\*24.5%\*\*

\- Ipswich win: \*\*31.6%\*\*

\- Most likely individual scoreline: \*\*1-1\*\*



The 1-1 prediction means that, when every individual score such as 1-0, 2-0, 2-1, 0-0, 1-1 and 1-2 is considered separately, 1-1 has the highest individual probability.



The 43.9% Crystal Palace win probability is calculated differently.  It combines the probabilities of \*\*every scoreline in which Crystal Palace win\*\*, rather than relying on one individual score.



For example, 1-0, 2-0, 2-1, 3-0 and 3-1 may each individually be less likely than 1-1.  When all possible Crystal Palace winning scores are added together, however, they account for 43.9% of the overall probability.  This is higher than the combined draw probability of 24.5% and the combined Ipswich win probability of 31.6%.



Model 2 therefore selects Crystal Palace as the 1X2 prediction even though 1-1 is the single most likely exact score.



The calculation is internally consistent.  The more important question is why \*\*1-1 is appearing so frequently as the most likely scoreline\*\*.



\## 1-1 scoreline concentration



GW4 has made this behaviour particularly visible:



\- GW1: \*\*8/10\*\* predictions had 1-1 as the most likely score

\- GW2: \*\*8/10\*\*

\- GW3: \*\*7/10\*\*

\- GW4: \*\*10/10\*\*

\- First 40 live predictions: \*\*33/40 (82.5%)\*\*



Across the 30 matches already evaluated in GW1-GW3, only \*\*4/30\*\* actually finished 1-1.



There is a related draw issue.  Across those same 30 evaluated fixtures:



\- Actual draws: \*\*10/30\*\*

\- Model 2 predicted draws by 1X2 outcome: \*\*0/30\*\*

\- 1-1 modal scorelines: \*\*23/30\*\*



This combination is important.  Model 2 is repeatedly identifying 1-1 as the most likely individual score while still not making a draw its overall 1X2 selection.



One likely area for investigation is the goal estimates feeding the scoreline model.  The GW4 expected-goal predictions are concentrated within a relatively narrow range, with every team estimated between 1.09 and 1.70 goals.  In this range, one goal for each team can frequently become the highest-probability individual score.



That does not mean the 1X2 outcome calculation is wrong.  It does suggest that the scoreline model may not be separating different types of fixtures strongly enough.



\## Why Model 2 remains unchanged



The purpose of the first five live Gameweeks is to test the frozen model prospectively rather than repeatedly change it in response to individual results.



Changing the model now would break that experiment and make it harder to distinguish genuine improvement from reacting to a small number of matches.



GW4 therefore remains an untouched Model2\_v1.0 prediction.



After GW5, the live sample will be approximately \*\*50 matches\*\*.  The international break that follows provides a natural review point to examine the full prospective evidence before making further modelling changes.



The review will look beyond headline accuracy.  It will examine where and why the model is succeeding or failing, including:



1\. draw discrimination and the difference between draw probability and 1X2 selection;

2\. the unusually high concentration of 1-1 modal scorelines;

3\. whether expected-goal estimates are too compressed across different fixtures;

4\. performance at different confidence levels;

5\. home and away prediction behaviour;

6\. promoted-team and cold-start performance;

7\. fixtures where Model 2 materially disagrees with the market; and

8\. other repeatable failure patterns visible across the prospective sample.



The objective will not be to force the model to predict more draws or produce a wider range of scorelines simply because the first few Gameweeks exposed those behaviours.  The prospective evidence will be used to identify the underlying weakness first.  The next challenger model can then be designed around a specific, testable hypothesis and evaluated against the frozen Model 2 benchmark.



This keeps the development process evidence-led: \*\*observe the live behaviour, identify the failure pattern, understand its cause, then test whether a targeted change genuinely improves out-of-sample performance.\*\*



\## What to watch in GW4



GW4 gives us another 10 untouched prospective predictions before the final Gameweek of the initial live evaluation period.



The most visible test is the 1-1 concentration.  All 10 fixtures have the same modal scoreline despite substantial differences in the teams and in the predicted 1X2 probabilities.



The results will therefore provide further evidence on whether the scoreline concentration is simply a feature of the underlying probability distributions or a meaningful limitation in Model 2's ability to distinguish goal-scoring patterns between fixtures.



No conclusions will be drawn from GW4 in isolation.  It will become part of the wider 50-match review after GW5.
