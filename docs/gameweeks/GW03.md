## What we learned

GW3 produced 3 correct 1X2 outcomes from 10 matches.  This was the weakest live Gameweek so far for Model 2.

Six of the ten GW3 matches ended level, while Model 2 again selected zero draws as its highest-probability 1X2 outcome.  7 of 10 fixtures had 1-1 as the single most likely scoreline, while 2 actually finished 1-1.

Five areas are being monitored through the initial live evaluation period:

1. **Draw discrimination** — Model 2 has now predicted zero draws by 1X2 argmax across the first 30 live fixtures, while 10 matches have actually ended level.
2. **1-1 modal scoreline concentration** — 23 of 30 live predictions have had 1-1 as the single most likely scoreline, while only 4 matches actually finished 1-1.
3. **Promoted-team cold-start performance** — the promoted-team subset performed strongly in GW3, but the sample remains too small for firm conclusions.
4. **Probability calibration and confidence** — Accuracy, Log Loss and Brier Score are all currently worse than the historical Model 2 validation benchmark and will continue to be monitored as the prospective sample grows.
5. **Incremental predictive value** — Model 2 will continue to be compared with simple historical baselines to determine whether its fixture-specific features consistently improve prediction quality.

The post-launch investigations have already tested several possible explanations for the observed behaviour.  Dixon-Coles adjustment, direct draw calibration and a direct nonlinear 1X2 classifier did not produce sufficient historical improvement to replace Model 2.  The observed-xG Model 5 challenger subsequently passed its historical development hurdle but failed the untouched 2025/26 out-of-time promotion test.

The evidence therefore does not support changing the production architecture in response to GW3.

Model 2 remains frozen as the official live benchmark through GW5.  After approximately 50 prospective predictions, the live evidence will be reviewed systematically by confidence, draw behaviour, promoted teams, home/away outcomes, market disagreement and other identifiable failure segments before deciding the next modelling hypothesis.
