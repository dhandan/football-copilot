## What we learned

GW2 produced 5 correct 1X2 outcomes from 10 matches.  This is broadly close to the historical Model 2 accuracy of 52.69%, but a single Gameweek is too small a sample to draw firm conclusions about model performance.

Five areas are being monitored through the initial live evaluation period:

1. **xG compression** — the model may be pulling team-strength estimates too strongly towards league-average scoring levels.
2. **1-1 modal scoreline concentration** — 8 of 10 fixtures had 1-1 as the single most likely scoreline, while 2 actually finished 1-1.
3. **Promoted-team pessimism** — cold-start performance will continue to be monitored as the live sample grows.
4. **Upset calibration** — lower-probability results will be tracked to determine whether early surprises represent normal football variance or a systematic model bias.
5. **Incremental predictive value** — Model 2 will continue to be compared with simple historical benchmarks to determine whether its fixture-specific features consistently improve prediction quality.

Model 2 remains frozen as the official live benchmark through the initial five-Gameweek monitoring period.  Challenger models may be developed and tested in parallel during this period, but they will not retrospectively alter the official Model 2 predictions.


## Post-GW2 Model Investigation: Model 3A

The completion of GW2 provided the first 20 prospective predictions from the frozen Model 2.

Although 1X2 accuracy remained at 50.0%, two behaviours warranted deeper investigation:

- Model 2 had selected Draw as the highest-probability 1X2 outcome in 0 of 20 fixtures, while 4 matches had actually ended level.
- 1-1 had been the modal exact score in 16 of 20 predictions, while only 2 matches actually finished 1-1.

Rather than changing Model 2 using only 20 live observations, I used the live behaviour to formulate a hypothesis that could be tested against a much larger historical sample.

### Hypothesis

Model 2 uses independent Poisson goal probabilities.

The hypothesis was that this independence assumption might inadequately represent low-scoring football outcomes, contributing to the unusual scoreline distribution and potentially poor draw calibration.

I therefore built **Model 3A**, a Dixon-Coles challenger.

Crucially, Model 3A retained exactly the same Model 2 features and expected-goal estimates. The only change was the application of a Dixon-Coles correction to the low-scoring cells of the probability matrix.

This created a controlled experiment rather than introducing several modelling changes simultaneously.

### Historical test

Model 3A was tested walk-forward across 1,100 matches from the 2023/24, 2024/25 and 2025/26 seasons.

The result was revealing.

| Metric | Model 2 | Model 3A |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accuracy | 52.18% | 52.18% |
| Log Loss | 1.0037 | 1.0041 |
| Brier Score | 0.5999 | 0.6001 |
| Predicted Draw Rate | 0.00% | 0.00% |
| Modal 1-1 Rate | 71.18% | 64.55% |

The historical sample contained 268 draws, representing 24.36% of matches.

Neither model selected Draw as its most likely 1X2 outcome in a single match.

Dixon-Coles did reduce the concentration of 1-1 modal scorelines, but it did not solve the underlying draw problem and marginally worsened both Log Loss and Brier Score.

### Decision

**Model 3A will not be promoted to prospective shadow deployment.**

This is an important result.

The live experiment successfully identified an unusual model behaviour, but rather than tuning the production model from a 20-match sample, the observation was converted into a testable hypothesis and evaluated against historical unseen data.

The evidence did not support promotion.

Model 2 therefore remains frozen while the next investigation focuses specifically on the relationship between expected-goal separation, total expected goals and draw probability.

## Model development after GW2

The live behaviour observed across GW1 and GW2 triggered a sequence of controlled historical experiments.

The purpose was not to modify the frozen Model 2 predictions retrospectively, but to use the live observations to formulate hypotheses that could be tested against historical unseen data.

### Model 3A: Dixon-Coles

The first challenger investigated whether Model 2's independent Poisson formulation inadequately represented dependence between low-scoring outcomes.

Across 1,100 historical walk-forward test fixtures, Dixon-Coles reduced the concentration of 1-1 modal scorelines from 71.18% to 64.55%.

However:

- Accuracy remained 52.18%.
- Log Loss deteriorated marginally.
- Brier Score deteriorated marginally.
- The model still predicted zero draws as the highest-probability 1X2 outcome.

**Decision: Model 3A rejected.**

### Model 3B: Draw calibration

The next experiment tested whether Model 2 contained useful information about draw likelihood but systematically positioned Draw too low relative to Home and Away.

A draw-probability multiplier was fitted using historical training data within the walk-forward process.

The fitted multiplier averaged approximately 0.992, effectively indicating that the optimiser did not support materially increasing Model 2's draw probabilities.

Across the 1,100 historical test fixtures:

| Metric | Model 2 | Model 3B |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Accuracy | 52.18% | 52.18% |
| Log Loss | 1.0037 | 1.0037 |
| Brier Score | 0.5999 | 0.5999 |
| Predicted draws | 0 | 0 |

**Decision: Model 3B rejected.**

This provided evidence that globally increasing Draw probability was not the solution.

### Model 4: Direct 1X2 Gradient Boosting

Rather than continue making small adjustments to the Poisson architecture, Model 4 deliberately tested a much larger structural change.

Model 4 predicted Home / Draw / Away directly using gradient boosting.

It combined the existing Model 2 information with:

- leakage-safe pre-match Elo
- relative team strength
- form acceleration
- attack and defence trends
- nonlinear feature interactions

Before running the experiment, a meaningful promotion threshold was defined:

| Metric | Model 2 reference | Model 4 target |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Accuracy | 52.18% | >= 54.00% |
| Log Loss | 1.0037 | <= 0.980 |
| Brier Score | 0.5999 | <= 0.585 |

The resulting historical walk-forward performance was:

| Metric | Model 2 | Model 4 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Accuracy | 52.18% | 51.27% |
| Log Loss | 1.0037 | 1.0319 |
| Brier Score | 0.5999 | 0.6158 |

Model 4 failed all three promotion thresholds.

It was also worse than Model 2 on Accuracy, Log Loss and Brier Score in each of the three historical test seasons.

Model 4 did begin predicting draws:

- 24 predicted draws
- 7 correct
- 2.61% draw recall
- 29.17% draw precision

However, changing the zero-draw behaviour did not improve overall model performance.

**Decision: Model 4 rejected.**

### What we learned

The three experiments tested increasingly different explanations for the behaviour identified during the live experiment:

1. **Model 3A:** Is low-score dependence the problem?  
   Result: not enough to improve performance.

2. **Model 3B:** Are Draw probabilities simply too low?  
   Result: the fitted adjustment was effectively neutral.

3. **Model 4:** Is the Poisson architecture itself the constraint?  
   Result: a direct nonlinear 1X2 classifier performed materially worse.

The evidence therefore suggests that further algorithm changes using essentially the same underlying match-history information are unlikely to deliver the material improvement being sought.

### Next phase: data enrichment

This closes the current algorithm-only experimentation phase.

The next research question becomes:

> Can genuinely richer football information improve predictive performance beyond Model 2?

The first priority will be genuine historical expected-goals data and related chance-quality information.

Potential enriched features include:

- rolling xG for and against
- xG difference
- home and away xG strength
- short- and medium-term xG form
- xG trend and acceleration
- goals versus xG over/underperformance
- shots and shots on target
- richer team-strength information
- player availability and line-ups where practical

Model 2 remains frozen as the official live 2026/27 baseline while this research takes place independently.
