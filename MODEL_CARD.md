# Football Copilot Model Card

## Production Model

**Model:** Model 2
**Version:** `Model2_v1.0`
**Method:** Enhanced Poisson regression
**Status:** Frozen production model / champion

Model 2 remains the production champion for Football Copilot.  The production model is frozen and must not be retrained or modified as part of the live 2026/27 gameweek evaluation process.

## Purpose

The model estimates Premier League match outcomes including:

* home win probability
* draw probability
* away win probability
* expected home goals
* expected away goals
* most likely scorelines

## Model Inputs

Model 2 uses pre-match historical football features.

These include:

### Five-match form

* home recent goals scored
* home recent goals conceded
* home recent points per game
* away recent goals scored
* away recent goals conceded
* away recent points per game

### Ten-match form

* home ten-match goals scored
* home ten-match goals conceded
* home ten-match points per game
* away ten-match goals scored
* away ten-match goals conceded
* away ten-match points per game

### Venue performance

* home-team home points per game
* home-team home scoring
* away-team away points per game
* away-team away scoring

### Season-to-date strength

* home season points per game
* home season goal difference per game
* away season points per game
* away season goal difference per game

### Relative strength

* recent PPG difference
* ten-match PPG difference
* season PPG difference
* home attack versus away defence
* away attack versus home defence

## Modelling Approach

Two Poisson regression models estimate expected goals for the home and away teams:

```text
Football features
      |
      +-- Home-goals Poisson model
      |
      +-- Away-goals Poisson model
```

The expected-goal estimates are converted into a score probability matrix.  This provides:

* expected home goals
* expected away goals
* home win probability
* draw probability
* away win probability
* most likely scoreline

The production workflow remains deterministic once the frozen model and pre-match features have been supplied.

## Production Governance

`Model2_v1.0` is the frozen champion model.

The live gameweek workflow must not:

* retrain Model 2 using current-season outcomes
* change the frozen model specification during prospective evaluation
* regenerate historical GW1-GW5 predictions using information that became available later
* overwrite an existing official gameweek prediction snapshot
* incorporate bookmaker probabilities directly into the champion model

Official Model 2 predictions are generated by:

```text
scripts/generate_live_gameweek_predictions.py
```

The official prediction files remain the source of truth for evaluating the champion.

## Opening Market Benchmark

Historical bookmaker opening prices were compared with Model 2 using the frozen Football-Data 2026/27 GW1-GW5 dataset.

The market benchmark uses:

```text
AvgH
AvgD
AvgA
```

These represent average opening decimal prices for:

```text
Home
Draw
Away
```

Raw implied probabilities are calculated as the inverse of the decimal odds and then normalised to remove the bookmaker overround:

```text
raw_home = 1 / AvgH
raw_draw = 1 / AvgD
raw_away = 1 / AvgA

total = raw_home + raw_draw + raw_away

market_home = raw_home / total
market_draw = raw_draw / total
market_away = raw_away / total
```

Closing-market fields `AvgCH`, `AvgCD` and `AvgCA` are not used for the opening-market challenger.

## Shadow Challenger

**Challenger:** `Model2_OpeningMarket_40_60_v1.0`
**Status:** Frozen shadow challenger
**Champion:** `Model2_v1.0`

The challenger combines the independent probability estimates from Model 2 and the opening bookmaker market.

The frozen blend is:

```text
40% Model 2
60% opening market
```

For each outcome:

```text
Blended probability =
    (0.40 x Model 2 probability)
    +
    (0.60 x market probability)
```

The challenger does not retrain or modify Model 2.  It operates as a separate shadow layer using the already-frozen Model 2 probabilities and an independently captured market snapshot.

## Challenger Selection

A development sweep was performed across Model 2 / market weights using GW1-GW5.

The strongest region was broad rather than a single sharp optimum, with approximately:

```text
35%-45% Model 2
55%-65% opening market
```

The 40% Model 2 / 60% market specification was selected and frozen within this region before prospective GW6 evaluation.

Development performance for the selected blend was:

| Gameweek    | Model 2 Accuracy | Market Accuracy | 40/60 Blend Accuracy |
| ----------- | ---------------: | --------------: | -------------------: |
| GW1         |              50% |             60% |                  60% |
| GW2         |              50% |             50% |                  50% |
| GW3         |              30% |             30% |                  30% |
| GW4         |              30% |             40% |                  40% |
| GW5         |              50% |             40% |                  50% |
| **Overall** |          **42%** |         **44%** |              **46%** |

The development sample contains only 50 matches.  The observed improvement is therefore treated as challenger evidence rather than proof that the blend is superior to the production champion.

## Prospective Market Source

From GW6 onward, the shadow challenger uses prospective bookmaker data captured through The Odds API.

Configuration:

```text
Sport: soccer_epl
Region: uk
Market: h2h
Odds format: decimal
```

For each fixture, only bookmakers supplying a complete Home / Draw / Away market are included.

Decimal prices are averaged separately across the available UK bookmaker panel:

```text
Average Home odds
Average Draw odds
Average Away odds
```

The three average prices are then converted into inverse-odds probabilities and normalised to remove the overround.

This mirrors the concept of the historical Football-Data `AvgH`, `AvgD` and `AvgA` benchmark, although the prospective bookmaker panel is not assumed to be identical to the historical Football-Data panel.

## Prospective Validation Protocol

Prospective validation begins with **GW6 of the 2026/27 Premier League season**.

The challenger weight is frozen at:

```text
Model 2 = 40%
Opening market = 60%
```

The weight must **not be retuned using GW6 or later results**.

A single official decision-point snapshot is taken for each gameweek approximately **24 hours before the first Premier League fixture of that gameweek**.

At the official T-24h snapshot:

1. refresh the gameweek fixture data
2. generate and freeze the official `Model2_v1.0` predictions
3. capture and freeze the UK bookmaker market snapshot
4. generate and freeze the `Model2_OpeningMarket_40_60_v1.0` shadow predictions

The market snapshot must not be refreshed after the official decision point for the purpose of that gameweek's prospective evaluation.

Both champion and challenger are therefore evaluated from information frozen at the same pre-match decision point.

## Shadow Challenger Pipeline

Market snapshot:

```text

```

Shadow prediction generation:

```text
scripts/generate_market_shadow_predictions.py
```

```text

```

The shadow prediction generator consumes:

```text
Frozen official Model 2 probabilities
                +
Frozen official market probabilities
                |
                v
        40/60 probability blend
                |
                v
Frozen shadow prediction
```

It does not call or retrain Model 2.

## Snapshot Safeguards

The prospective pipeline includes controls intended to prevent accidental contamination of the live evaluation.

These include:

* official Model 2 predictions must already exist before an official shadow prediction can be generated
* the expected `Model2_v1.0` model version is validated
* the expected `Model2_OpeningMarket_40_60_v1.0` challenger version is validated
* exactly ten Premier League fixtures are expected for a standard gameweek
* fixture and team joins are validated
* probability totals are validated
* official prediction snapshots cannot be silently overwritten
* test market snapshots are kept separate from official snapshots
* test snapshots must not be used for prospective performance evaluation

## Evaluation Metrics

Champion and challenger outcome probabilities are evaluated using:

### Outcome accuracy

Whether the highest-probability Home / Draw / Away outcome matched the actual result.

### Log loss

Measures the probability assigned to the outcome that actually occurred.

Lower is better.

### Multiclass Brier score

Measures the squared error across the full Home / Draw / Away probability distribution.

Lower is better.

Model 2 additionally produces expected goals and scoreline predictions.  Those outputs remain champion-specific and are not attributed to the market blend.

## Interpretation

The opening market is treated as an external benchmark and complementary probability signal rather than as a replacement for the football model.

GW1-GW5 showed that:

* the market was narrowly stronger than Model 2 on aggregate outcome probability metrics
* Model 2 remained stronger than the market in some individual gameweeks
* the two signals disagreed on a meaningful subset of fixtures
* a simple frozen blend produced better aggregate development-period metrics than either signal independently

Because the evidence is based on only 50 development matches, the 40/60 blend remains a **shadow challenger**.

`Model2_v1.0` remains the production champion unless future prospective evidence supports a formally governed model change.

## Model Limitations

Important limitations include:

* football outcomes contain substantial randomness
* draws remain difficult to predict
* promoted or newly observed teams can require cold-start handling
* injuries, suspensions and expected line-ups are not directly modelled
* tactical changes are not explicitly represented
* historical form can lag sudden changes in team strength
* bookmaker markets contain information not represented in the football model
* the prospective market bookmaker panel may differ from the historical Football-Data panel
* GW1-GW5 challenger evidence is based on a small 50-match development sample

Probabilities should therefore be interpreted as estimates rather than certainties.
